# Philips layout usage guide

Choose the layout from the slide's communication job, not from color alone. Preserve the layout-defined title area and all master/footer objects. The body area remains flexible.

## Fast decision path

1. Opening the deck: use a title-slide layout (12–20).
2. Marking a new chapter with little supporting detail: use a divider (22–29).
3. Presenting text or evidence with a clear one-, two-, or three-part structure: use 34–38.
4. Letting one image carry the message: use 39.
5. Building a chart, process, timeline, dense evidence page, or other custom composition: use 30–33 when a title is needed, or 40 when it is not.
6. Closing the deck: use 41–44.

Do not add a divider merely to demonstrate template variety. A short deck may use no divider. Do not force a comparison into a single-column page or split one coherent argument into artificial columns.

## Covers: layouts 12–20

Use one cover only unless the user asks for multiple title pages. Keep it sparse: title, optional subtitle, and essential metadata.

- 12 `Title slide - blue (vertical logo)`: default Philips corporate cover; strongest brand opening.
- 13 `Title slide - white (vertical logo)`: restrained or formal cover with more whitespace.
- 14 `Title slide - light blue (vertical logo)`: softer, consumer-facing or experience-led opening.
- 15 `Title slide - blue`: wide horizontal-logo cover for short titles.
- 16 `Title slide - white`: bright horizontal-logo cover.
- 17–20 `Title slide: 1/3 logo …`: use only when the cover has a strong right-side image or visual asset. Choose the background variant for contrast with that image.

Avoid using cover layouts as chapter dividers or body pages.

## Dividers: layouts 22–29

Use dividers to mark a real change of chapter. Keep the page to a chapter number or short label, one title, and at most one clarifying line.

- 22–25 `Divider … (with image)`: use when a chapter has a meaningful image that sets context or mood.
- 26–29 text-only dividers: use when the section name itself is sufficient.
- Blue is the default brand emphasis; white is restrained; light blue feels exploratory or consumer oriented; dark blue suits technical depth or a major transition.

Do not place analysis, multiple bullets, or detailed evidence on a divider.

## Structured content: layouts 34–38

Use these when the information pattern genuinely matches the layout.

- 34 `Standard text`: one coherent argument, executive summary, definition, or a short sequence. Body content may mix paragraphs, a small table, or one supporting visual.
- 35 `Two content`: a true comparison, before/after, opportunity/response, or action/risk. The two sides need not have identical word counts.
- 36 `Three content`: three comparable stages, options, pillars, or audiences. Use only when all three carry similar weight.
- 37 `Content small - image right`: explanation or evidence on the left, one important image on the right.
- 38 `Content small - image left`: one important image on the left, interpretation or implications on the right.

Existing body placeholders provide useful starting geometry. They may be resized, replaced, or supplemented inside the body region when that improves clarity. Keep the title zone, safe margins, and footer clear.

## Visual and free-composition pages: layouts 30–33, 39–40

- 39 `Full bleed image`: one dominant photograph or visual with minimal text. Preserve crop quality and legibility.
- 30–33 `Title only`: use for charts, timelines, maps, process diagrams, mixed text-and-image storytelling, or any composition that needs a standard title but does not match 34–38. Match the background color to the narrative role and contrast needs.
- 40 `Footer only`: maximum freedom for a full-page chart, framework, or visual. Add a title only if the page still reads clearly and the custom title aligns with the normal title zone.

Free composition does not mean free brand placement. Never move or recreate the logo, copyright line, page number, or watermark.

## Endings: layouts 41–44

- 41/43 `Thank you`: use when a visible closing message is needed.
- 42/44 `End slide`: use for a minimal logo-only close.
- Blue is the standard branded ending; white is quieter and works after a light deck.

## Body-layout freedom

The following are fixed unless the user explicitly requests otherwise:

- canvas size;
- master and selected layout;
- title anchor and title color;
- background color;
- logo, copyright, page number, footer, and watermark anchors.

The following are flexible inside the body region:

- number and width of text columns;
- text hierarchy below the title;
- image count, crop, and placement;
- charts, tables, timelines, diagrams, and callouts;
- emphasis color drawn from the template palette;
- font family when the template font is unavailable.

Keep content editable. Prefer one clear composition over a grid of decorative cards.

## Example selection for a five-slide strategy report

1. Cover: 12 or 15.
2. Strategic conclusion: 34 for one argument, or 35 for opportunity versus positioning.
3. Technical evolution: 36 when it has three comparable generations; otherwise 30–33 for a custom timeline.
4. Consumer or cultural story: 37/38 when supported by one image, or 32 for a flexible light-blue composition.
5. Action plan and guardrails: 35, or 31 when the plan needs a custom timeline plus notes.
